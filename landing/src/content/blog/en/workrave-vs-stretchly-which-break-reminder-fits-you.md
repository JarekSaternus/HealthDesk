## What Are Workrave and Stretchly?

I remember the first time a colleague showed me his screen mid-afternoon — he had a small sheep animation pop up demanding he rest his eyes. That was Workrave, and back then it felt almost comically insistent. Years later, I came across Stretchly, which took a far quieter, more elegant approach to the same problem. Two tools, same mission, very different personalities.

### Overview of Workrave as an open-source break reminder

Workrave is a free, open-source break reminder built primarily for Windows and Linux, and it's been around since the early 2000s. Its original focus was RSI (repetitive strain injury) prevention, which still shapes how opinionated it is about actually making you stop — not just suggesting it.

The interface is dated by modern standards. But "dated" doesn't mean ineffective. Workrave gives you granular control over timers, postpone limits, and guided exercises — features that more polished apps sometimes strip away in the name of simplicity. If you want something that holds you accountable rather than gently suggesting a stretch, Workrave will do exactly that.

### Introduction to Stretchly and its core features

Stretchly launched around 2016 and has steadily built a loyal following. It runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and its main draw is a clean, distraction-minimizing design. Breaks arrive as full-screen or mini overlays with brief wellness tips — no animated sheep, no guilt-tripping.

What makes Stretchly stand out is how unobtrusive it tries to be. The defaults are sensible enough that many users never touch the settings at all, which is either a feature or a limitation depending on how much control you want.

### Why break reminder apps matter for desk workers

Sitting for long stretches without movement isn't just uncomfortable — a [2012 study published in Diabetologia](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-012-2677-z) found that prolonged sitting is independently associated with poor metabolic health, even in people who exercise regularly. That finding stuck with me because it reframes the problem entirely: it's not just about how much you move overall, but whether you actually break up the sitting itself.

For desk workers logging six to ten hours at a screen, that distinction matters enormously. A break reminder app is one of the lowest-friction interventions available — no gym membership, no wearable required. It's almost suspiciously simple.

## How Do Workrave and Stretchly Compare in Features?

On paper, both tools do the same thing: remind you to take breaks. But the moment you dig into configuration, the gap between them becomes obvious — and it cuts in both directions depending on what you actually need.

### Break scheduling and customization options

Workrave gives you three distinct break types with independently adjustable timers, while Stretchly separates breaks into "mini breaks" and "long breaks," each fully configurable. Both cover the fundamentals, but Workrave lets you set hard limits on how many times a break can be postponed — a detail that sounds minor until you're the kind of person who clicks "skip" seventeen times in a row.

Stretchly's scheduling is more flexible in one specific way: it supports natural break detection, meaning it won't interrupt you if you've already stepped away from your desk. That's a small but genuinely useful touch, and honestly one I wish Workrave had adopted by now.

### Exercise library and stretch demonstrations

Here's where things get interesting. Workrave includes a built-in library of guided exercises — illustrated postures and stretches displayed during rest breaks. They're not glamorous, but they're functional and actually tell your body what to do, which turns out to matter more than you'd think.

Stretchly, by contrast, shows short wellness tips and reminders during breaks rather than guided movement sequences. You're told to stretch your neck; you're not shown how. For someone already familiar with desk stretches, that's fine. For someone just starting to pay attention to ergonomics, Workrave's guided approach is meaningfully more useful.

### User interface and ease of use comparison

Let's be honest — Workrave looks like it was designed in 2003, because it largely was. The interface is functional but visually clunky, and first-time setup can feel like configuring a router. Stretchly, built with Electron, has a modern dark-mode-friendly UI that most users will find intuitive within minutes.

That said, the tradeoff is real. Stretchly's simplicity occasionally tips into limitation — there are things Workrave can configure that Stretchly simply can't. I'll admit, at first I dismissed Workrave's complexity as unnecessary friction. Then I realized the people who stick with it long-term are usually the same ones who actually follow through on break compliance.

If you want a break tool that also functions as a wellness coach with structured exercises and enforced stops, Workrave's friction is a feature, not a bug.

## Pricing and Accessibility

### Is Workrave Free to Use?

Both apps are free — but in meaningfully different ways. Workrave is fully open-source under the GPL license: zero cost, no account required, no feature gating whatsoever. You download it, install it, and everything is unlocked immediately. For anyone skeptical of subscription software or working in environments with strict IT policies, that kind of unconditional access genuinely matters.

The tradeoff is that development is community-driven and sporadic. Updates arrive when volunteers have time, not on a roadmap. The Windows build feels more polished than it used to, but the app still carries the aesthetic of something built in the early 2000s — which, well, it was.

### What Does Stretchly Cost?

Stretchly follows a freemium model. The core app is free and open-source ([GitHub](https://github.com/hovancik/stretchly)), but a voluntary "Plus" tier unlocks additional themes and customization options. Nothing health-critical is paywalled, which is a genuinely honest approach for a wellness tool.

In practice, most users never need to pay anything. The free tier covers break scheduling, natural break detection, and all the core functionality. If you want to support the solo developer behind it, the Plus tier costs a small one-time amount — but the majority of users will use it indefinitely for free without missing anything essential.

### Platform Compatibility: Where Does Each App Run?

| Platform | Workrave | Stretchly |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | ✅ | ✅ |
| macOS | ❌ | ✅ |
| Linux | ✅ | ✅ |

For macOS users, this table makes the decision for you. Workrave simply doesn't run on Mac and hasn't for years — Stretchly is the only option of the two. Linux users get both, and Workrave actually has stronger roots there, available through most major package managers including `apt` and `dnf`.

## Which App Offers Better Health Features?

### Does Either App Include Eye Exercise Routines?

This is where the gap between the two tools becomes most visible. Workrave includes a structured 20-20-20 rule prompt during rest breaks — reminding you to look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds — along with illustrated eye relaxation exercises. It's not glamorous, but it's grounded in [well-established ophthalmological guidance](https://www.aoa.org/healthy-eyes/eye-and-vision-conditions/computer-vision-syndrome) for reducing digital eye strain.

Stretchly doesn't offer guided eye exercises. It surfaces occasional text tips during breaks — "look away from your screen" type reminders — but there's no structured routine, nothing and no progression. For someone spending eight-plus hours staring at a monitor, that's a meaningful gap.

### How Do They Handle Posture and Ergonomics?

Workrave's illustrated micro-pause exercises include basic posture cues — neck rolls, shoulder shrugs, wrist stretches — displayed with simple stick-figure animations. A 2021 review in the [International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18052510) found that even brief, prompted movement breaks reduce musculoskeletal discomfort in desk workers. This suggests the content doesn't need to be elaborate to be effective.

Simple prompts, done consistently, actually work. Stretchly leans on break frequency over exercise content. The philosophy seems to be: step away from your screen regularly, and you'll naturally shift position. That works for some people.

From my experience, though, users who already have poor posture habits tend to resume exactly the same position the moment they sit back down — without any actual guidance to interrupt that pattern.

### What About Integration With Health Tracking?

Neither app integrates with external health platforms or wearables in any meaningful way. Workrave tracks your daily computer time and can log it locally, but there's no sync to Apple Health, Google Fit, or similar services. Stretchly's natural break detection is smart, but it still doesn't connect outward to broader health data.

If activity monitoring and hydration tracking alongside break reminders matter to you, a tool like [HealthDesk](https://healthdesk.site/en/) fills that gap more comprehensively than either of these apps can on their own.

## User Experience and Customization

The best break reminder is the one you don't ignore after day three.

### Notification Styles and Distraction Levels

Workrave takes a firm approach: when a break is due, it locks the screen. You can configure it to allow skipping, but the default behavior is deliberately disruptive. Some people need exactly that. Others find it maddening mid-sentence — and I'd be lying if I said I'd never force-quit it during a deadline.

Stretchly is gentler by design. A translucent overlay appears with a stretch suggestion, and you can postpone or skip with minimal friction. From my experience, this works better for people in flow-heavy work — writing, deep coding sessions — because the interruption feels less punishing. The compliance rate stays higher simply because the app isn't fighting you.

### Personalization Options for Different Work Schedules

Workrave gives you granular control: micro-break intervals, rest-break intervals, and a daily limit — all adjustable independently. Stretchly's customization has grown significantly since its early versions; you can now adjust break frequency, duration, and whether breaks are strict or skippable. The Plus tier unlocks additional break types and themes.

What neither app handles well is *irregular* schedules — the kind where you work intensely for 90 minutes, disappear for a meeting, and come back to a desk. Both tools work best with predictable, continuous sessions. Sound familiar? It's the one scenario most knowledge workers actually live in.

### Desktop Integration and System Resource Usage

Workrave runs as a lightweight background process with virtually no impact on system performance — a genuine advantage if you're on an older machine or running Linux with limited resources. Stretchly, built on Electron, carries a heavier footprint; community benchmarks on GitHub have noted it using roughly 150–200MB of RAM at idle. This won't destabilize a modern machine, but it's not negligible.

Neither app integrates with calendar tools to pause automatically during scheduled meetings — a gap that remains surprisingly unaddressed across most tools in this space.

### Workrave for Open-Source Enthusiasts and Linux Users

If you run Linux as your daily driver or care deeply about open-source software, Workrave is still a solid choice after all these years. It's configurable at a level most users never need — but that depth matters if you want precise control over micro-pause timing, break enforcement, or daily usage limits. Stretchly wins when simplicity is the priority. Setup takes minutes, the prompts are calm and unobtrusive, and you won't spend an evening reading documentation.
